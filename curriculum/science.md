## علوم — subject `science`

Lessons in `js/lessons/science.js`, science step types (`test`, `shadow`, `home`) in `js/steps/science.js`, English
versions in `js/en/science.js`. Starts at the child's Persian garden (`B.levelOf`). Inquiry first: look, guess, try,
explain; guesses are never marked wrong. Every lesson ends with a safe experiment at home (`home`) with everyday things
only: no fire, sharp tools, mains electricity or chemicals (a grown-up does any heating). Seed and Sprout children may
not read yet, so pictures, narration and the parent's questions carry the lesson. Examples from Iran's nature: the
Hyrcanian (Caspian) forests, Zagros oaks, the Lut desert, the Persian Gulf and its mangroves, Lake Urmia, qanats.

**Boundaries with other subjects.** Hygiene, healthy eating, safety and first aid belong to `health` (so «سالم باش،
شاداب باش», grade 1 lesson 3, is taught there); science covers how the body works, what food is made of and how
microbes live. Units of measurement are taught in `math`; science uses them. Maps and Iran's geography are in `world`;
science covers its nature, climate and rocks. Feelings about earthquakes and storms are in `feel`; science keeps them
calm and factual.

**Review still to do.** A science teacher reviews every lesson before it is published (`policies.html#accuracy`), and
should check this plan first: the questions are under [For a science teacher](#for-a-science-teacher) at the end.

### Frameworks

**The best systems and best practice lead.** What is taught, and in what order, follows the science standards of the
systems that do best in TIMSS and PISA science, and the strongest research on how children learn science. Where they
differ, the plan chooses what the evidence supports and says why ([Where the benchmarks differ](#where-the-benchmarks-differ)).
The Iranian curriculum is used in two ways only: to fill the `School` column, so «همراه مدرسه» can follow a child's
class, and where Iranian schools clearly cover more than the benchmarks at the same stage, to add some of it, usually as
`full`. Where the Iranian curriculum covers the same or less, the benchmarks alone decide. Iran's nature stays the
local context for examples.

- **Benchmarks** (codes in the `School` column are NGSS performance expectations; England, Singapore and Japan are
  named by year or grade where they decided a depth or a place):
  - *NGSS* (NGSS Lead States, 2013) and *A Framework for K-12 Science Education* (National Research Council, 2012):
    performance expectations K–8, the three dimensions (practices, crosscutting concepts, disciplinary core ideas) and
    the seven crosscutting concepts: patterns; cause and effect; scale, proportion and quantity; systems and models;
    energy and matter; structure and function; stability and change. It gives the backbone and the order K–5.
  - *England*: National curriculum in England, science programmes of study, key stages 1–3 (Department for Education,
    2013, updated 2015): a year-by-year list from age 5, the clearest check of what is taught at each age.
  - *Singapore*: Primary Science Syllabus (Ministry of Education, 2023; themes Diversity, Cycles, Systems, Energy,
    Interactions, taught as a spiral from Primary 3) and the Lower Secondary Science Syllabus (2021).
  - *Japan*: Course of Study for elementary and lower secondary school, science (MEXT, 2017; science from grade 3,
    «life environment studies» in grades 1–2; w3id.org/jp-cos).
  - *Korea* (2022 revised national curriculum: integrated «Wise Life» in grades 1–2, science from grade 3), *Finland*
    (National Core Curriculum 2014: environmental studies grades 1–6) and *Estonia* (national curriculum 2011:
    natural science from grade 1): all start with integrated nature study before age 8–9 and separate science after,
    which is the Seed–Sprout–Sapling shape here.
  - *Early years* (Seed): Early Years Foundation Stage, «Understanding the world» (England, 2021) and the Head Start
    Early Learning Outcomes Framework, «Scientific reasoning» (USA, 2015).
- **Research.**
  - *Big ideas*: Harlen (ed.), *Working with Big Ideas of Science Education* (ASE, 2015).
  - *Inquiry*: predict, observe, explain (White & Gunstone, *Probing Understanding*, 1992); guided rather than open
    inquiry for young children (Furtak, Seidel, Iverson & Briggs, *Review of Educational Research* 82(3), 2012);
    Education Endowment Foundation, *Improving Primary Science* (2023): practical work with a clear purpose, talk,
    vocabulary taught explicitly, misconceptions addressed.
  - *Learning progressions*: matter (Smith, Wiser, Anderson & Krajcik, *Measurement* 4(1–2), 2006), energy (Neumann,
    Viering, Boone & Fischer, *JRST* 50(2), 2013), and Duschl, Schweingruber & Shouse (eds.), *Taking Science to
    School* (National Academies, 2007); misconceptions: Driver, Squires, Rushworth & Wood-Robinson, *Making Sense of
    Secondary Science* (1994).
  - *Safe home experiments*: ASE, *Be Safe!* (4th ed., 2011), and CLEAPSS guidance for primary science: only kitchen
    materials, a grown-up for anything hot, nothing tasted unless it is food.
  - *Iran's nature*: سازمان حفاظت محیط زیست (Asiatic cheetah, Persian leopard, protected areas); UNESCO World Heritage
    files for the Hyrcanian forests (2019), the Lut desert (2016) and the Persian qanat (2016).

### Where the benchmarks differ

| Topic | What the systems do | Chosen here, and why |
|---|---|---|
| Science before age 8 | NGSS and England from age 5; Japan, Korea, Finland, Estonia and Singapore integrate it in nature study until 8–9 | Seed and Sprout are short, play-based nature study with the NGSS K–1 ideas as `core`: the content is light, and early guided inquiry with talk is what the EEF and Furtak reviews support |
| Water cycle | NGSS at middle school; England Year 4, Singapore P4, Japan grade 4 (ages 9–10) | `core` in Tree (`science.tree.17`); the built `science-sapling-1` is `full` in Sapling. Most systems teach evaporation and condensation at 9–10, once states of matter are known |
| Food chains | England Year 4, NGSS grade 5, Singapore P5–6, Japan grade 6 | `core` in Tree (`science.tree.25`), `full` in Sapling (`science.sap.25`, the built `science-sapling-7`) |
| Grouping vertebrates | England Year 1 (names the five groups), Singapore P3; not in NGSS | `core` in Sapling (`science.sap.14`): three of the benchmarks teach it by age 8 |
| Light and sound | NGSS grade 1; England Year 3 (light) and Year 4 (sound); Japan grade 3 | A first `core` look in Sprout (`science.spr.20`, NGSS order), deepened as `core` in Sapling (`science.sap.8`, `science.sap.9`) |
| Earth's spin explains day and night | NGSS grade 5, England Year 5, Japan grade 6; Iran grade 2 | Sprout and Sapling observe the patterns (`core`); the explanation is `core` in Tree (`science.tree.16`), `full` in Sapling (`science.sap.7`) |
| Particles | NGSS grade 5; England and Singapore at 11–14 | `core` in Tree (`science.tree.4`): the matter progression (Smith et al., 2006) supports "tiny pieces too small to see" and conservation of mass at 10–11 |
| Simple machines | England Year 5, Japan grade 6; not in NGSS | `core` in Tree (`science.tree.11`); the lever in Sapling is `full` |
| Separating mixtures, dissolving | England Year 5, Japan grade 5, Singapore lower secondary; NGSS only identifies substances | `core` in Tree (`science.tree.2`) and Forest; dissolving in Sapling is `full` |
| Evolution | England Year 6 (fossils, variation, adaptation), NGSS middle school | `full` in Tree (`science.tree.33`), `core` in Forest (`science.for.38`) |
| Waves as waves | NGSS grade 4; England, Singapore and Japan at 11–14 | `full` in Tree (`science.tree.30`), `core` in Forest (`science.for.42`): only one benchmark teaches it at 9–10 |
| Acids, alkalis, pH | England key stage 3, Singapore lower secondary, Japan grade 6; NGSS only as properties | `core` in Forest (`science.for.24`) |
| Subatomic particles, bonding | Iran grades 8–9; England and NGSS at 14–16 | `full` in Forest (`science.for.15`, `science.for.25`) |
| Pressure, work and power | England key stage 3, Singapore lower secondary, Iran grade 9; not in NGSS middle school | `core` in Forest (`science.for.31`, `science.for.32`) |
| Hormones, classification of animal groups at 12–15 | Iran grades 8–9; England key stage 4 or primary | `full` in Forest (`science.for.17`, `science.for.36`) |

### Strands

| Strand | What grows along it |
|---|---|
| Living things | living and non-living → plants and animals, their parts and needs → life cycles, groups, habitats, traits → body systems, cells, microbes → heredity, reproduction, ecosystems, evolution |
| Matter | looking at and sorting materials → properties, uses, changes that can be undone → states, dissolving, mixtures → physical and chemical change, particles → atoms, elements, reactions |
| Forces and energy | push and pull → light, sound, forces, magnets → heat, electricity, machines, waves → motion, Newton's laws, pressure, energy transfer and conservation |
| Earth and space | weather and the sky → land, water, seasons, weather and climate → rocks, soil, fossils, the solar system, Earth's systems → plate tectonics, Earth's history, climate, space |
| Engineering | build and try → design, test, improve → criteria and constraints, compare solutions → optimise, models; how technology changes (the textbooks' «از گذشته تا آینده») |
| Practices | look with the senses, guess, try → fair test, measure, record → variables, tables and graphs, conclusions → models, evidence, argument, uncertainty |

### 🌰 دانه — `seed`

Looking closely at the world around us.

**Size:** 2 years × 36 weeks × 2 sessions of about 10 minutes = 144 sessions: **24 lessons** and 120 sessions of review,
practice games and home explorations (2 short sessions a week fit the Seed day of 20–30 minutes for every subject).

### 🌲 جنگل — `forest`

Models and evidence.

**Size:** 3 years × 36 weeks × 4 sessions = 432 sessions: **135 lessons** (the units below) and 297 sessions of review,
practice and investigations (NGSS middle school, England key stage 3, Singapore lower secondary, Japan lower secondary;
school grades 7–9 have 4 hours of «علوم تجربی» a week). NGSS and England band middle school rather than fix an order
by year, so the units follow the order of the Iranian grade 7–9 books, which lets «همراه مدرسه» follow the class.
Iranian topics beyond the benchmarks (subatomic particles, bonding, fuels, hormones, animal groups) are `full`. Each
row is the first lesson of its unit; the unit's other lessons get their own rows and ids when the garden is planned in
detail.

### For a science teacher

Questions this plan leaves to a teacher's judgement:

1. **The benchmark choices.** Check the table under [Where the benchmarks differ](#where-the-benchmarks-differ), above
   all the water cycle and food chains moving to Tree as `core` (the built Sapling lessons stay, as `full`), and
   evolution as `core` in Forest (it is in NGSS and England but not a chapter in the Iranian books).
2. **Textbook numbering.** The `School` column for grades 1–9 comes from published chapter lists, not the PDFs; check
   it against chap.sch.ir (especially grade 1 «از خانه تا مدرسه» as motion, and grades 2 and 3 where one textbook
   lesson has two parts).
3. **«همراه مدرسه».** Iranian grade 1–3 topics such as water, air, rocks, floating and dissolving are now `full`. A
   child in school still meets them there; does the parent setting show `full` objectives of the chosen grade too?
4. **Earthquakes, floods and storms.** They are taught calmly and practically (what happens, what to do). Is that right
   for children who have lived through war and disasters, and how should `feel` and `health` link to it?
5. **Home experiments.** Every home task uses kitchen things only, with a grown-up for anything hot. Check the Tree and
   Forest ones (a solar cooker, a red-cabbage indicator, electromagnets with a battery) when they are written.
6. **Overlap with `health`.** The human body is split: how it works here, keeping it healthy in `health`. Check
   `science-sapling-37`, `science-tree-13`, `science-tree-30` and `science-forest-5` against the health plan.
