## سلامت و ایمنی — subject `health`

Lessons in `js/lessons/health.js`, step types `doit` (do something real with the body, away from the screen) and
`forparents` (the note that ends every lesson) in `js/steps/health.js`. Health, the body, movement and safety from
the Seed garden to the end of the Forest garden: hygiene and illness, food and water, sleep, movement and physical
activity, the body and growing up, body safety, safety at home, on the road, online and in emergencies, first aid,
and the links to mental health. Never frightening: practical, calm and reassuring, with no war, injury or violence
imagery (`policies.html`, «ایمنی کودک»); dangers are shown as the safe action, not the accident.
Feelings themselves belong to `feel`; how the body works (organs, systems, cells) to `science`; this subject is
what the child *does* to stay healthy and safe.

**Needs review before launch** (see "Review" at the end): a health professional (a paediatrician or school health
nurse, and a physical education teacher for the movement lessons) for every health fact, dose, number and
movement; a child-protection specialist for every body-safety, bullying, online-safety and growing-up lesson, the
words said to the child and the parent notes.

### Frameworks

**The best systems and WHO, UNICEF and UNESCO guidance lead.** They decide what is taught and in what order.
The Iranian curriculum only fills the School column (see "Iran" below). Health and PE are benchmarked against:

- **England.** *Relationships Education, Relationships and Sex Education and Health Education* statutory guidance
  (Department for Education, 2019, in force from 2020; [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/relationships-education-relationships-and-sex-education-rse-and-health-education)).
  Health education in primary covers mental wellbeing, internet safety and harms, physical health and fitness,
  healthy eating, drugs, alcohol and tobacco, health and prevention (sun, teeth, sleep, vaccines, allergies), basic
  first aid (a clear emergency call, common injuries including head injuries) and "changing adolescent body" (key
  facts about puberty, including menstruation, before the end of primary). Secondary adds CPR and defibrillators.
  *National curriculum in England: PE programmes of study*, key stages 1–3 (2013): fundamental movement, balance,
  agility and coordination, games, dance, gymnastics, athletics, outdoor and adventurous activity, and swimming
  (25 metres and self-rescue by the end of primary).
- **Finland.** *National core curriculum for basic education 2014* (Finnish National Agency for Education): PE in
  every grade (motor skills, physical activity for well-being, social and emotional skills in play), and health
  education (terveystieto) as its own subject in grades 7–9: growth and development, health literacy, lifestyle,
  mental health, substances, safety, evaluating health information.
- **Singapore.** *Physical Education syllabus* (Ministry of Education, primary and secondary, 2014): athletics,
  dance, games and sports, gymnastics, outdoor education, swimming, and physical health and fitness and safety
  across all of them.
- **Canada.** *The Ontario Curriculum, Health and Physical Education, Grades 1–8* (2019): Active Living, Movement
  Competence and Healthy Living (healthy eating, personal safety and injury prevention, substance use, human
  development and sexual health), with social-emotional skills in every strand; correct names for body parts from
  grade 1.
- **Australia.** *Australian Curriculum: Health and Physical Education* v9.0 (ACARA, 2022): "Personal, social and
  community health" and "Movement and physical activity", with focus areas for safety, food and nutrition, alcohol
  and drugs, relationships and sexuality, mental health, active play, fundamental movement skills, rhythmic and
  expressive movement, challenge and adventure, lifelong physical activity and aquatics.
- **United States.** *National Health Education Standards* (SHAPE America, revised 2024): functional knowledge,
  influences on health, finding valid information, communication, decision-making, goal-setting, self-management
  and advocacy. *National Physical Education Standards* (SHAPE America, 2024): motor skills, knowledge of movement
  and fitness, social and emotional skills, and valuing physical activity.

International guidance, used with them:

- **WHO and UNESCO, health-promoting schools.** *Making every school a health-promoting school: global standards and
  indicators* (WHO and UNESCO, 2021): the curriculum supports physical, social-emotional and psychological health,
  safety, physical activity and healthy nutrition
  ([unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000377948](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000377948)).
- **Physical activity and sleep.** *WHO guidelines on physical activity, sedentary behaviour and sleep for children
  under 5 years of age* (2019): 180 minutes of activity a day at 3–4, at least 60 of them moderate to vigorous,
  little sitting, 10–13 hours of sleep
  ([who.int news, 24 April 2019](https://www.who.int/news/item/24-04-2019-to-grow-up-healthy-children-need-to-sit-less-and-play-more)).
  *WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour* (2020): 5–17 years, an average of 60 minutes a day
  of moderate to vigorous, mostly aerobic activity, with vigorous and muscle- and bone-strengthening activity on at
  least 3 days a week, and less recreational screen time
  ([WHO fact sheet, 2024](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity)). Sleep hours come
  from the American Academy of Sleep Medicine consensus (2016): 3–5 years 10–13 hours, 6–12 years 9–12, 13–18 years
  8–10 ([Paruthi et al., PMC5078711](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5078711/)).
- **Body safety, growing up, relationships.** UNESCO *International technical guidance on sexuality education*
  (revised 2018, with UNAIDS, UNFPA, UNICEF, UN Women and WHO;
  [PDF](https://www.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/pub-pdf/ITGSE.pdf)). It is used **only** for what is
  age-appropriate in body safety, hygiene and general growing up:
  - key concept 4, violence and staying safe: 4.1 recognising abuse and bullying, that it is never the child's fault,
    saying «نه» and telling a trusted adult; 4.2 body rights and private parts at 5–8, privacy and unwanted attention
    at 9–12; 4.3 safe use of the internet;
  - key concept 6: 6.3 puberty (at 5–8, that growing up brings changes and this is normal; at 9–12, the main
    physical and emotional changes, hygiene, periods as normal and not shameful, and finding reliable information);
    6.4 body image (all bodies are different and good).

  The lessons are calm and factual, stay within `policies.html` and say nothing about sexual behaviour. For the
  youngest children they also use the Council of Europe's "Underwear Rule" (Kiko and the Hand).
- **First aid.**
  - IFRC *International first aid, resuscitation and education guidelines* (2020;
    [ifrc.org](https://www.ifrc.org/document/international-first-aid-resuscitation-and-education-guidelines)).
  - The Belgian Red Cross–Flanders evidence-based educational pathway, which says which first-aid skills children
    can learn at which age: calling for help from preschool, wounds, burns and bleeding in primary, the recovery
    position and chest compressions from about 12
    ([KU Leuven](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/bitstream/123456789/569942/1/leerlijnen.pdf),
    [Cochrane abstract](https://abstracts.cochrane.org/2015-vienna/evidence-based-educational-pathway-integration-first-aid-training-school-curricula)).
  - The European Resuscitation Council's *Kids Save Lives* statement, endorsed by WHO (2015): two hours of CPR
    training a year from age 12.
  - Burns: cool running water for 20 minutes, no ice (paediatric cohort, *Annals of Emergency Medicine* 2020; the
    reviewer confirms this against the Iranian Red Crescent's current course).
- **Disasters.**
  - UNICEF's preparedness work ([unicef.org/iran](https://www.unicef.org/iran/%D8%A2%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AF%DA%AF%DB%8C-%D9%88-%D9%BE%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%AE-%D8%A8%D9%87-%D8%B4%D8%B1%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%B7-%D8%A7%D8%B6%D8%B7%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B1%DB%8C))
    and the Red Crescent's family preparedness advice: a family plan, a meeting place, an emergency bag.
  - The earthquake drill «بنشین، پناه بگیر، محکم نگه دار», the international "drop, cover, hold on".
  - Strange objects: the risk-education message used by UNICEF and the mine-action standard IMAS 12.10: don't touch,
    step back, tell a grown-up
    ([UNICEF Syria](https://www.unicef.org/syria/stories/learning-stay-safe-children-participate-explosive-ordnance-risk-education)).
    It is taught for every strange object (needles, batteries, a firework that did not go off), with no war context.
  - For Forest, WHO *Psychological first aid: guide for field workers* (2011): look, listen, link.
- **Road safety.** WHO *Ten strategies for keeping children safe on the road* (2015): helmets, child restraints and
  seat belts, seeing and being seen, and supervision near roads. 38% of children killed on roads are pedestrians
  ([WHO EMRO](https://www.emro.who.int/violence-injuries-disabilities/violence-infocus/savekidslives.html)).
- **Online safety.** ITU *Guidelines for child online protection* (2020), with materials for children under 9,
  9–11 and 12–18 ([ITU](https://www.itu.int/en/mediacentre/Pages/pr10-2020-Guidelines-Child-Online-Protecion.aspx)),
  and ITGSE key concept 4.3.
- **Food, drowning, substances.**
  - WHO *Healthy diet* fact sheet: water, fruit and vegetables, less free sugar and salt. No body-shape or diet talk
    with children.
  - WHO *Preventing drowning: an implementation guide* (2017): supervision, barriers, basic swimming and
    water-safety skills.
  - UNODC/WHO *International standards on drug use prevention* (2nd ed., 2018): life skills and refusal skills in
    early adolescence, not scare tactics.

**Where the systems differ, and what Baghcheh does:**

- **Names for private parts.**
  - What the systems do: Ontario (grade 1) and England (primary) teach the correct names, and child-protection
    practice favours them because a child who has the words can tell. Many Iranian families avoid them.
  - What Baghcheh does: the lessons say that private parts have names and that the child can use them with trusted
    grown-ups. The parent note gives the correct Persian names and says why they help. The child-protection reviewer
    settles the wording.
- **When puberty is taught.**
  - What the systems do: England (before the end of primary), Ontario (grades 4–6) and ITGSE (9–12) teach it before
    most children start puberty; it can begin at 8.
  - What Baghcheh does: the Tree garden (9–12), in general terms, with personal care in Forest.
- **Dance, gymnastics, outdoor activity, swimming.**
  - What the systems do: England, Singapore, Australia and SHAPE America all teach rhythmic and expressive
    movement, gymnastics and outdoor activity. Iranian PE is mostly games and fundamental skills.
  - What Baghcheh does: it adds movement concepts, moving to a beat, body shapes and balances (home-safe: no
    handstands or flips) and safe outdoor activity. Swimming can't be taught on a screen, so Baghcheh teaches water
    safety and self-rescue knowledge, and the parent note recommends lessons.
- **CPR.**
  - What the evidence says: *Kids Save Lives* and England's secondary curriculum.
  - What Baghcheh does: hands-only CPR is `core` in Forest.
- **Drugs, alcohol and tobacco.**
  - What the systems do: England and Australia start in primary.
  - What Baghcheh does: Tree introduces medicines, tobacco, hookah and energy drinks (`full`), and Forest teaches
    refusal skills (`core`), following the UNODC standards.
- **Influences and advocacy.**
  - What the evidence says: the NHES standards (and Australia's "community health") ask children to analyse
    influences such as advertising and friends, and to speak up for health.
  - What Baghcheh does: a Tree lesson on influences and a Forest lesson on advocacy, both `full`.
- **Online safety.**
  - What the systems do: England, Australia and the ITU guidelines start it in the first school years.
  - What Baghcheh does: `core` from Sprout.

**Iran.** The Iranian national curriculum (برنامه درسی ملی, 1391) and the school textbooks are used only for the
School column, so «همراه مدرسه» can follow a child's class.

- **Where health is taught in Iranian schools.** Iran has no separate health textbook before upper secondary.
  Health and safety are taught inside other subjects:
  - تربیت بدنی (physical education): about 120 minutes a week in primary, about 6% of the timetable
    ([rimag.ir/Article/41636](https://rimag.ir/Article/41636/FullText)), or two periods a week
    ([hamshahrionline.ir/news/710649](https://www.hamshahrionline.ir/news/710649)).
  - علوم تجربی (science): grade 5 lesson 5 «حرکت بدن»; grade 7 chapter 12 «سفره سلامت» and chapter 13 «سفر غذا».
  - تفکر و سبک زندگی (grades 7–8): habits and self-care ([ensani.ir/fa/article/577298](https://www.ensani.ir/fa/article/577298)).
  - آمادگی دفاعی (grade 9): lesson 10 «پدافند غیرعامل», civil protection and safety in disasters. Only its safety and
    first-aid content is matched here.
- **School programmes.**
  - The yearly national earthquake and safety drill, «مانور سراسری زلزله و ایمنی»: late آذر, every grade, run by
    IIEES with the Ministry of Education and the Iranian Red Crescent
    ([iiees.ac.ir, 26th drill](https://www.iiees.ac.ir/en/the-26th-national-earthquake-and-safety-drill-november-27-2024/),
    [PreventionWeb case study](https://preventionweb.net/files/61513_csspolicycasestudyirannationwidesch.pdf)).
  - The health check before grade 1, «سنجش سلامت بدو ورود به دبستان»: sight, hearing, teeth.
- **School column.** Textbook lesson numbers change between editions, so the ones we could not check in the current
  book are marked "(to confirm)".
- **Nothing added from Iranian schools.** No Iranian school topic covers more than the benchmark systems, so none
  was added as `full`. The Iran-specific dangers are a different thing: balconies and windows, gas and carbon
  monoxide from heaters, motorbikes, the school van, water tanks, dust and polluted air, floods, and fireworks at
  Chaharshanbe Suri. They are there because WHO injury-prevention guidance says to teach the risks children
  actually meet where they live, not because a textbook covers them.
- **Help numbers.**
  - Taught to children: ۱۱۵ (اورژانس), ۱۲۵ (آتش‌نشانی), ۱۱۰ (پلیس).
  - For parents and older children: ۱۲۳ (اورژانس اجتماعی بهزیستی: child abuse, family crises;
    [asriran.com](https://www.asriran.com/fa/news/789609)).
  - To confirm with the reviewer before use: ۱۱۲ (امداد و نجات هلال احمر), ۱۴۸۰ (صدای مشاور بهزیستی), ۱۹۰
    (اطلاع‌رسانی داروها و سموم).

### 🌰 دانه — `seed`

**Sizing:** 2 years × 36 weeks × 2 sessions a week (the 2 PE periods of the Iranian timetable; Seed sessions are
10–15 minutes) = 144 sessions: 23 lessons here and the 5 for every garden (28 sessions of new teaching), and about
116 sessions of practice and review, most of them the daily movement routine. The movement itself (WHO: 180 minutes
of play a day, 60 of them lively) happens off screen.

### Step types needed

| Step type / option | What it does | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| `move` | **Built** (`js/steps/health.js`). A guided movement routine: one move at a time, shown by a person moving in a `scene` (`js/art/rig-health.js`), with the number of times said aloud at a calm pace (counting, never a timer or a score), an easier and a harder version, a rest with a slow breath between moves, warm-up first and calm-down last.
