## ریاضی — subject `math`

Goal: a full mathematics course from first counting (Seed) to the end of grade 9 (Forest): number sense through
objects and pictures (Seed, Sprout), place value and the four operations with quick facts (Sprout, Sapling),
fractions, decimals, ratio and percent, measures and geometry (Sapling, Tree), then integers, algebra, proof,
statistics and probability (Forest). Concrete → pictorial → abstract throughout: ten-frames, bundles of ten, the
number line, arrays and the area model, fraction strips, the bar model, the balance, with examples from Iranian
daily life (sangak bread, the bazaar, tomans, the Persian calendar, carpets and tiles).

Expert review still to do: a primary math teacher (Seed to Sapling) and a lower-secondary math teacher (Tree,
Forest) check the objectives, their order and the School column against the current textbooks; see "For a math
teacher to decide" at the end.

### Frameworks

**The best systems and best practice lead.** What is taught, and in what order, follows the countries that do best in
TIMSS and PISA mathematics and the strongest research, not the Iranian books. The Iranian curriculum is used in two
ways only (see "Iran" below). The benchmarks:

- Singapore: *Primary Mathematics Syllabus, Primary One to Six* (Ministry of Education, 2021) and *Secondary
  Mathematics Syllabuses* (2020, Express / G3);
- Japan: *Course of Study* (学習指導要領) for elementary 算数 and lower-secondary 数学 (MEXT, 2017; in force 2020–21);
- Korea: the *2022 revised national mathematics curriculum* (Ministry of Education);
- Hong Kong: *Mathematics Education Key Learning Area Curriculum Guide (Primary 1 – Secondary 6)* (Curriculum
  Development Council, 2017);
- Estonia: *National Curriculum for Basic Schools*, Appendix 3 "Mathematics" (2011, with later amendments);
- the top results these rest on: *TIMSS 2023 International Results in Mathematics and Science* (IEA, 2024: Singapore,
  Chinese Taipei, Korea, Hong Kong and Japan lead at grades 4 and 8) and *PISA 2022 Results, Volume I* (OECD, 2023:
  Singapore, Macao, Chinese Taipei, Hong Kong, Japan, Korea and Estonia lead in mathematics).

Where these systems and Common Core put a topic in different years, the plan chooses as follows (and why):

| Topic | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers to 10,000, × of 2–3 digits by 1 digit, remainders | Sapling, `core` | Singapore P3, Japan and Korea grade 2–3 do it (and Iran grade 3); Common Core waits to grade 4. Fluent place value early helps later written methods |
| Times tables | Sapling, `core`, all by the end | Every benchmark finishes them by grade 3 |
| Circle: circumference and area | Tree, `core` | Singapore P6, Japan and Korea grade 6 (and Iran grade 6); Common Core grade 7 |
| Letters for numbers, simple equations | Tree, `core` | Japan grade 6, Singapore P6, Common Core grade 6 |
| Negative numbers | Tree as a `full` preview, Forest `core` | Common Core grade 6, but Japan, Singapore, Korea and Iran grade 7; the later systems score higher, and the number line in context is enough before |
| Probability as a number | Tree `full`, Forest `core` | Common Core grade 7, Japan grade 8; Iran grade 4–5 is earlier than the evidence suggests is useful |
| Line and point symmetry; turns and slides | symmetry Tree `core`; transformations Tree `full`, Forest `core` | Japan grade 6 (symmetry), Common Core grade 8 and Japan grade 7 (transformations) |
| Proof with congruent triangles, inscribed angles, Pythagoras, expanding and factorising | Forest, `core` | Japan and Korea grades 8–9 (and Iran grades 8–9); Common Core keeps formal proof for high school, but the top systems show grade 8–9 children manage it |
| Quadratic equations | Forest, `full` | Japan, Korea and Singapore (Sec 2–3) by grade 9; Iran and Common Core later |
| Line plots, box plots, scatter plots, sampling | Sapling line plots `full`; Forest `core` | Common Core grades 2–8 and Japan grades 7–9 (histograms, box plots, sampling); missing from the Iranian books |
| Bar model for word problems | from Sapling, `core` | Singapore's model method; the strongest evidence we know for word problems (Ng & Lee 2009) |

**Iran (the School column, and some extras).** Every objective names the Iranian grade and textbook chapter it
matches (the School column), so a child can move between Baghcheh and school and a parent using «همراه مدرسه» can
follow the class. Where the Iranian books clearly cover more than the benchmarks at the same stage, some of it is added
as `full` (divisibility tests in Tree, vectors, sets and rational expressions in Forest). Where they cover the same or
less, the benchmarks decide alone.

**Research and frameworks.**

- *Common Core State Standards for Mathematics* (NGA Center & CCSSO, 2010), K–8 domains (Counting and Cardinality,
  Operations and Algebraic Thinking, Number and Operations in Base Ten, Number and Operations—Fractions, Measurement
  and Data, Geometry, Ratios and Proportional Relationships, The Number System, Expressions and Equations,
  Functions, Statistics and Probability) and the eight Standards for Mathematical Practice; and the
  *Progressions for the Common Core State Standards* (Common Core Standards Writing Team, 2011–2013,
  Institute for Mathematics and Education, University of Arizona): Counting and Cardinality, K–5 OA, K–5 NBT, 3–5
  NF, 6–7 RP, 6–8 EE, 6–8 SP, K–6 Geometry. The order of facts, place value and fractions follows these.
- Singapore's methods (the syllabus above): the
  concrete–pictorial–abstract sequence, the bar model for word problems (Kho Tek Hong; Ng Swee Fong & Lee Kerry,
  "The model method", *Journal for Research in Mathematics Education* 40(3), 2009), number bonds, mental strategies.
- *Principles and Standards for School Mathematics* (NCTM, 2000), *Curriculum Focal Points for Prekindergarten
  through Grade 8* (NCTM, 2006) and *Principles to Actions* (NCTM, 2014): the content strands and the process
  standards (problem solving, reasoning and proof, communication, connections, representation).
- Early number: *Mathematics Learning in Early Childhood: Paths Toward Excellence and Equity* (National Research
  Council, 2009); Clements & Sarama, *Learning and Teaching Early Math: The Learning Trajectories Approach*
  (Routledge, 2nd ed. 2014): subitising, counting, comparing, composing numbers and shapes; Siegler & Ramani
  (2008, 2009), linear number board games improve number sense (`math-seed-31`); Frye et al., *Teaching Math to
  Young Children* (IES Practice Guide NCEE 2014-4005, 2013).
- Fact fluency through strategies and spaced practice: Baroody, "Why children have difficulties mastering the basic
  number combinations" (*Teaching Children Mathematics* 13(1), 2006); Fuchs et al., *Assisting Students Struggling
  with Mathematics: Intervention in the Elementary Grades* (IES Practice Guide WWC 2021006, 2021); Rohrer & Taylor,
  "The shuffling of mathematics problems improves learning" (*Instructional Science* 35, 2007) on interleaving;
  Dunlosky et al. (2013) on spacing and retrieval (see `method.html`).
- Fractions: Siegler et al., *Developing Effective Fractions Instruction for Kindergarten Through 8th Grade* (IES
  Practice Guide NCEE 2010-4039, 2010): fractions as numbers on the number line from the start.
- Problem solving and the move to algebra: Woodward et al., *Improving Mathematical Problem Solving in Grades 4
  Through 8* (IES Practice Guide NCEE 2012-4055, 2012); Star et al., *Teaching Strategies for Improving Algebra
  Knowledge in Middle and High School Students* (IES Practice Guide NCEE 2015-4010, 2015): worked examples,
  comparing solution methods, the structure of expressions; Kaput, Carraher & Blanton, *Algebra in the Early
  Grades* (2008): «=» as a relation and the unknown from Sprout on; Knuth et al. (2006) on the equal sign.
- International benchmark: the *TIMSS 2023 Assessment Frameworks* (IEA, Mullis, Martin & von Davier eds., 2021): grade 4 content
  domains Number, Measurement and Geometry, Data; grade 8 Number, Algebra, Geometry, Data and Probability. Iran takes part in TIMSS.

### Strands

| Strand | What grows | Seed → Forest |
|---|---|---|
| number (عدد و ارزش مکانی) | Counting, numerals, place value, integers, rational and real numbers, powers | counting to 10 → 100 → 1,000 → billions → integers → real numbers and scientific notation |
| operations (عمل‌ها) | Meaning of +, −, ×, ÷; facts by strategies; mental and written methods | stories with fingers → facts to 20 → times tables → long division → order of operations |
| fractions (کسر و اعشار) | Equal parts, fractions as numbers on the line, decimals and their operations | halves → unit fractions on a line → equal fractions → four operations → rational numbers |
| ratio (نسبت و درصد) | Ratio, rate, proportion, percent | — → (doubling recipes) → ratio tables, percent → inverse proportion, percent change |
| algebra (الگو و جبر) | Patterns, «=» as balance, unknowns, expressions, equations, lines | repeating patterns → the missing number → input–output rules → equations → lines and systems |
| geometry (هندسه) | Shapes and solids, position, angles, symmetry and transformations, proof | naming shapes → polygons and right angles → angles and quadrilaterals → proof, Pythagoras, vectors |
| measurement (اندازه‌گیری) | Length, mass, capacity, time, money, perimeter, area, volume | bigger and smaller → units → cm, kg, l, the clock → area and volume formulas → solids |
| data (آمار و احتمال) | Sorting, graphs, averages, chance, sets | sorting → picture graphs → bar charts and chance → mean and probability → statistics, sets |
| problem solving (حل مسئله) | Word problems, models (bar model), strategies, explaining and proving | number stories → bar models → textbook strategies → writing a solution and a proof |

Problem solving is also inside every unit: each unit ends with word problems, and the textbook's «حلّ مسئله» pages
(draw a picture, make a table, find a pattern, guess and check, work backwards, simplify) are taught as strategies
in Sapling and Tree.

### 🌰 دانه — `seed`

Before school: counting and how many, seeing small numbers at a glance, comparing, shapes, positions, sorting and
patterns, all with things and pictures; first number stories with fingers and objects. No written sums yet.

**Size:** 2 years × 36 weeks × 3 math sessions a week (about 10 minutes, so 6 of the garden's 20–30 minutes a day)
= 216 sessions: **50 lessons** (one session in four, a little more in the first weeks) and **166 practice sessions**
(reviews of mastered lessons and the generators below). Core depth: the 36 core lessons and about half the practice.

### 🌱 جوانه — `sprout`

The year before school and the first school year: numbers to 20 with ten-frames, then counting to 100; adding and
subtracting to 10 until it is quick, and to 20 by counting on and doubles; «=» as "the same as"; writing digits and
number sentences; shapes and solids; measuring with units; time and the week; first data.

**Size:** 2 years × 36 weeks × 4 math sessions a week (about 10 minutes of the garden's 30–45 a day) = 288 sessions:
**60 lessons** and **228 practice sessions** (fact fluency to 10 needs a lot of short, spaced practice). Core depth:
the core lessons and about half the practice.

### 🌿 نهال — `sapling`

The second and third school years: facts to 20 made quick; place value to 1,000, then 10,000; adding and
subtracting with regrouping, first with bundles, then written in columns; multiplication and division from equal
groups and arrays to the times tables; unit fractions and fractions on the number line; centimetres, kilograms,
litres, the clock to the minute, money; perimeter and area; bar charts and chance; the bar model for word problems.

**Size:** 2 years × 36 weeks × 5 math sessions a week (about 12 minutes of the garden's 45–60 a day) = 360 sessions:
**99 lessons** and **261 practice sessions** (times-table and fact fluency are practice, not lessons). Core depth:
the core lessons and about half the practice.

### 🌳 درخت — `tree`

The fourth to sixth school years: numbers to millions and billions; multi-digit multiplication and long division;
fractions (equal fractions, the four operations, mixed numbers) and decimals; ratio, proportion and percent; angles,
quadrilaterals, the circle, area and volume; symmetry and turns; units and converting; line graphs, the mean and
probability; first letters for numbers. Every unit starts from a model (area model, fraction strips, the number
line, the bar model) and ends with a written method.

**Size:** 3 years × 36 weeks × 5 math sessions a week (about 15 minutes of the garden's 60–90 a day) = 540 sessions:
**about 170 lessons** and **about 370 practice sessions**. The rows below are every objective's first lessons
(51 of them); the rest of each unit is written when the garden is built, in the same units. Core depth: the core
lessons and about half the practice.

### 🌲 جنگل — `forest`

The seventh to ninth school years (دوره اول متوسطه): integers, rational and real numbers, powers and roots; from
expressions to equations, inequalities, lines and systems; reasoning and proof in geometry (parallel lines, triangles,
congruence, similarity, Pythagoras, the circle); vectors and coordinates; areas and volumes of solids; sets,
statistics and probability. Algebra grows out of arithmetic the child already knows: the balance, the bar model and
patterns become letters.

**Size:** 3 years × 36 weeks × 5 math sessions a week (about 20 minutes of the garden's 90–120 a day) = 540 sessions:
**about 180 lessons** and **about 360 practice sessions**. The rows below are every objective's first lessons
(49 of them); the rest of each unit is written when the garden is built, in the same units. Core depth: the core
lessons and about half the practice.

### For a math teacher to decide

- **Notation must match the school books.** Iranian textbooks write decimals with a slash (۲/۵), set long division
  out in their own layout (the divisor to the right of the dividend, the quotient under the divisor), and use their own terms (شمارنده،
  ب.م.م، ک.م.م، عدد مخلوط، مدل نواری is not a textbook term). The `column`, `bar` and fraction steps must follow
  the textbook layout; a teacher should confirm each one before the step is built (and `STYLE.md` should say it).
- **The placement check skips Tree.** A child who can do ۶۸۴ ÷ ۴ starts in Forest, but Tree now holds fractions,
  decimals, ratio and percent (grades 4–6). A Tree → Forest item (such as ¾ + ⅔ or ۲۰٪ of ۳۵۰) should be added to
  `B.PLACEMENT` in `js/content.js`; that is a change to `CURRICULUM.md`'s "Outside lessons", not to this file.
- **The bar model.** It is the strongest tool we know for word problems (Singapore), but Iranian books teach
  «رسم شکل» more loosely. Keep it as Baghcheh's main model from Sapling on, or use it only at full depth?
- **Where the times tables go.** The old plan put them in Tree; Iranian grade 3 (and Common Core grade 3) teach them,
  so they are now in Sapling (`math-sapling-63` to `math-sapling-69`), with Tree only reviewing the slow facts.
- **Core or full.** The benchmark choices are in the table under "Frameworks"; the ones a teacher should confirm:
  probability and transformations only `full` in Tree although Iranian grades 4–6 teach them; proof, inscribed
  angles and factorising `core` in Forest; quadratic equations `full` in Forest (ahead of Iranian school); sets,
  vectors and rational expressions `full` although the Iranian grade 7–9 books teach them.
- **Different years from school.** With the benchmarks leading, some topics come earlier or later than in the
  Iranian books (four-digit numbers and remainders earlier; probability later). A parent using «همراه مدرسه» sees
  the school grade's objectives first in any case; should Baghcheh warn when an objective is from a later garden?
- **Calculators.** None are planned. Should Forest allow one for big real-world data and percent problems?
- **Seed and Sprout boundary with school.** Built Sapling lessons start with make-ten and two-digit numbers (school
  grade 1 content), so Sprout ends in the middle of the grade 1 book. A parent using «همراه مدرسه» for grade 1 sees
  objectives from both gardens; the teacher should check this split.
- **Pace.** The sizes assume 3, 4, 5, 5 and 5 math sessions a week. A teacher should check that about 50, 60, 100,
  170 and 180 ten-minute lessons, plus practice, really cover each stage at full depth.
