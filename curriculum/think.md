## فکر و برنامه‌نویسی — subject `think`

Lessons in `js/lessons/thinking.js` (English versions in `js/en/thinking.js`), step types `code` (block coding on a
grid) and `unplugged` (a screen-free thinking game for the parent) in `js/steps/coding.js`. Logical thinking before
code, and unplugged before plugged: patterns, sorting, sequences and conditions in everyday life first, then programs
with arrows, turns and repetition, then conditions, functions and variables in blocks, then text programming
(Python, as in England's key stage 3, Finland's grades 7–9 and Iranian schools) in the Forest garden. A program that misses is something to fix, never a loss
(debugging is the skill). Every lesson ends with an `unplugged` game, so the idea moves off the screen.

The subject also carries **digital literacy and safety** for devices, accounts and information: how computers and
the internet work, passwords and privacy, checking what is true, screen balance and artificial intelligence. Safety
with *people* online (strangers, personal information, telling a grown-up) is taught in سلامت و ایمنی
(`health-tree-2`); feelings about screens and sleep in سلامت و ایمنی (`health-forest-2`) and احساس‌های من. Charts
and tables are taught in ریاضی (`math-sapling-20`, `math-tree-18`); here data is about *information*: codes,
storing, collecting and asking questions of it.

### Frameworks

**The best systems and best practice lead** (CURRICULUM.md, "Scope and sequence"): what is taught and in what order
comes from the curricula of strong systems and from computing-education research and frameworks. Iran's «کار و
فناوری» and «تفکر و پژوهش» are used only for the School column, plus a few `full` additions where they clearly cover
more (listed below).

**Benchmark systems and frameworks**

- **CSTA K–12 Computer Science Standards (revised 2017)**, the most detailed progression: Level 1A (ages 5–7), 1B
  (ages 8–11), 2 (ages 11–14), in five concepts (computing systems; networks and the internet; data and analysis;
  algorithms and programming; impacts of computing). Standard codes such as `1A-AP-10` are given in "Can do" so a
  reviewer can check them. Level 1A starts at Sprout, 1B spans Sapling and Tree, Level 2 is Forest.
- **England, national curriculum for computing (DfE, 2013)**, compulsory from age 5. Key stage 1 (5–7): algorithms,
  simple programs and debugging, predicting with logical reasoning, storing and retrieving digital content, safe
  use and keeping personal information private. Key stage 2 (7–11): sequence, selection, repetition, variables,
  input and output, decomposing problems, controlling or simulating physical systems, networks and the internet,
  searching well and how results are ranked, judging digital content. Key stage 3 (11–14): at least one textual
  language, data structures (lists), procedures and functions, key sorting and searching algorithms, Boolean logic,
  binary numbers and binary addition, how text, sound and pictures are stored, how a computer stores and runs
  instructions, protecting online identity and privacy.
- **Finland, national core curriculum for basic education (2014, in use from 2016)**: programming inside mathematics
  and as a transversal skill: sequences of instructions tested by the pupil in grades 1–2, a working program in a
  visual environment by grade 6, simple programs with computational thinking by grade 9 (typically ScratchJr, then
  Scratch, then Python).
- **Estonia, ProgeTiiger (since 2012)**: programming and robotics from kindergarten (floor robots), Scratch and
  robots in primary school, almost all schools and kindergartens taking part; informatics is an elective subject.
- **Korea, 2022 revised national curriculum**: informatics doubled (34 hours in primary grades 5–6, more in middle
  school), with AI and software education for every pupil, rolling out 2024–2027.
- **Singapore, Code for Fun (MOE and IMDA)**: a compulsory 10-hour coding and computational-thinking course for every
  upper-primary pupil since 2020, with block programming and simple physical computing.
- **Barefoot Computing** (Computing At School / BT, UK) for computational thinking: six concepts (logic, algorithms,
  decomposition, patterns, abstraction, evaluation) and five approaches (tinkering, creating, debugging,
  persevering, collaborating), and its early-years material for Seed.
- **CS Unplugged** (Bell, Witten and Fellows, University of Canterbury, 1998 and csunplugged.org): binary numbers,
  image representation, error detection, searching, sorting and sorting networks, graphs, routing and protocols,
  taught without a computer. Baghcheh's `unplugged` step and many `scene` animations come from it.
- **Blocks to text**: Weintrop and Wilensky (“To block or not to block”, 2015; “Between a block and a typeface”, IDC
  2017; Weintrop, Bain and Wilensky, “Blocking progress?”, 2017): block-based programming helps beginners at first
  and does not hold them back later, and hybrid block/text views ease the move; Lin and Weintrop's “Switch mode”
  (University of Maryland, 2024).
- **Digital literacy and safety**: Common Sense Education's Digital Citizenship Curriculum (media balance and
  well-being, privacy and security, digital footprint and identity, relationships and communication, cyberbullying,
  news and media literacy); ITU Child Online Protection Guidelines for children (2020).
- **Artificial intelligence**: AI4K12 “Five Big Ideas in AI” (perception, representation and reasoning, learning,
  natural interaction, societal impact) and its grade-band progression charts (AAAI and CSTA); UNESCO “AI competency
  framework for students” (2024: human-centred mindset, ethics of AI, AI techniques and applications, AI system design).

**Where good systems differ, and what Baghcheh does**

- **When programming starts.** England, Finland, Estonia and CSTA start at 5–7; Singapore and Korea later. Baghcheh
  starts at Seed with arrows and floor games, as Estonia and Barefoot's early years do, because the earliest steps
  are sequencing and directions, which young children learn well unplugged and on tiny grids.
- **When text programming starts.** England requires a textual language in key stage 3 (11–14) and Finland's grades
  7–9 usually use Python; CSTA does not name a modality. The research above finds blocks a good start and a hybrid
  view a good bridge. So blocks run to the end of Tree, each block program's text is shown beside it from the end of
  Tree, and Python starts in Forest.
- **Lists, Boolean logic, binary, sorting and searching.** England's key stage 3 requires them for everyone; CSTA puts
  lists and some of the rest at Level 3A. Baghcheh follows England: they are `core` in Forest.
- **Artificial intelligence.** CSTA 2017 hardly mentions it; Korea's 2022 curriculum and UNESCO's 2024 framework
  make it part of every pupil's learning. So AI is `full` in Tree (what it is) and `core` in Forest (using it wisely).
- **Physical computing.** England's key stage 2 and Singapore's Code for Fun use real or simulated devices. Baghcheh is
  offline and screen-only, so it simulates them (a traffic light, a lamp and switches) and leaves robot kits to
  families who have them.

**Iranian curriculum: School column, and the `full` additions it brings.** In primary school there is no computing
before grade 6. Additions where Iranian schools clearly cover more than the benchmarks: flowcharts at Tree
(«الگوریتم و روندنما» in grade 6; CSTA has them at Level 2), touch typing in Persian and English (grade 6), and
everyday digital tools and public online services («مستندسازی», «اشتراک‌گذاری اطلاعات», «شهروند الکترونیکی»);
all three are `full`. The School column matches:

  گردآوری اطلاعات», «طرح پرسش پژوهشی», «از کجا بدانم؟», «ارائه یافته‌های پژوهشی»), «نظام‌مندی», «هویت و ارزش».
- **کار و فناوری، پایه هفتم** (2 hours a week). Required modules: ۱ نوآوری و فناوری، ۲ کاربرد فناوری اطلاعات و
  ارتباطات، ۳ جست‌وجو و جمع‌آوری اطلاعات، ۴ مستندسازی، ۵ اشتراک‌گذاری اطلاعات، ۶ برنامه‌نویسی (Python: `print`,

  using Python for maths and science problems), «شهروند الکترونیکی ۱ و ۲» (email, online public services),
  «الکترونیک», and modules outside this subject.
- **کار و فناوری، پایه نهم** (2 hours a week): a Python programming module (loops; reported as module 4) beside

### Strands

| Strand | What grows |
|---|---|
| logic | Same and different, sorting by one rule then two, odd one out, if… then…, not, and/or, true and false, truth tables and logic gates |
| patterns | Repeating, growing and number patterns; spotting the repeating part of a path or a program (the root of loops and abstraction) |
| algorithms | Steps in order, precise instructions, decomposing a task, comparing two ways, sorting and searching, flowcharts and pseudocode, counting steps |
| programming | Arrows → forward and turn → repeat → conditions, events, functions, variables, pen drawing (blocks) → Python: variables, input, if, loops, lists, functions (text) → projects |
| debugging | Fix one wrong block → run, find, fix, test again → trace step by step → test cases, error messages |
| data | Symbols stand for things, picture codes, pixels, binary, character codes and colours; collecting, organising and questioning data; spreadsheets and models |
| systems | Devices and their parts, input and output, hardware and software, files and folders, how the internet carries messages, troubleshooting |
| digital | Using devices with grown-ups, passwords, private information, kindness online, screen balance, searching and checking information, digital footprint, AI, technology and society |
| puzzles | Picture puzzles, mazes, clue puzzles, logic grids, sudoku, river crossings, graphs; explaining how you solved it |

### 🌰 دانه — `seed`

Sizing: 2 years × 36 weeks × 1 session a week (about 10 minutes, within the Seed day of 20–30 minutes) = 72 sessions:
**14 lessons** and 58 practice and review sessions (each mastered lesson comes back about five times, the rest is
generated practice). The anchor is Barefoot's early years and Estonia's kindergarten floor robots (Iranian schools teach none of this before grade 6).

### 🌿 نهال — `sapling`

Sizing: 2 years × 36 weeks × 2 sessions a week (about 15 minutes, about a tenth of the Sapling day of 45–60
minutes) = 144 sessions: **27 lessons** and 117 practice and review sessions (generated code boards are most of the
practice). CSTA Level 1A and England's key stage 1 are finished, and 1B and key stage 2 begin (Iranian schools teach none of it yet).
